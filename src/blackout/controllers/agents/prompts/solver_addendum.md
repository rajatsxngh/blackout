## Your planning solver

You also have a planning solver: an OR-Tools CP-SAT model that plans power first (which zones to shed, the order each crew repairs substations, when each hospital gets grid power back) and then fuel (every truck's next one or two trips over the next 120 minutes, minimizing hospital-minutes dark, earlier darkness weighted more). It plans from **your** belief table, so it is only as good as what you've recorded. It knows nothing you haven't told it.

- `call_solver(overrides)` returns a plan: an ID, each truck's trips with their ETAs, the predicted dark minutes per hospital, the shed set, each substation's return and the crew orders, and the actions that would start the plan now. `overrides` lets you plan with corrections laid over your belief without recording them: roads to treat as blocked or open, a hospital's fuel or burn rate, a hospital's priority weight, trucks to leave idle, or a truck pinned to a hospital. Pass empty lists for what you don't want to override. You can call it at most **2 times per decision point**, so record your beliefs first, and use the second call only to compare an alternative.
- `accept_plan(plan_id, reason, edits?)` carries out a plan from this decision point: its proposed actions are applied immediately, in order, and each result comes back. `edits.skip` lists truck, crew or zone IDs whose proposed actions you want left out (then act on those yourself). Your `reason` is written in front of each action's own reason in the replay log.

The solver never acts on its own: nothing happens until you accept a plan or act yourself. It's strong at the arithmetic (deadlines, travel times, how much fuel goes where) and weak where your judgment is needed: which reports to believe, and anything it can't see. Check its plan against what you know before you accept it.
